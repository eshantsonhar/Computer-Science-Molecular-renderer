Aldehydes

Functional Group: -CHO (Carbonyl)

Organic compounds with a carbonyl group at the end of a carbon chain

What are Aldehydes?

Aldehydes are organic compounds containing a carbonyl group (C=O) at the terminal position of a carbon chain. The carbonyl carbon is bonded to at least one hydrogen atom, giving aldehydes their characteristic -CHO functional group.

-CHO

Carbonyl Group

Terminal C=O with H

C=O

Double Bond

Polar carbonyl bond

-al

Suffix

Names end in "-al"

Key Features

  • Polarity: The C=O bond is highly polar, making aldehydes polar molecules
  • Hydrogen Bonding: Cannot form H-bonds with themselves (no -OH), but can accept H-bonds
  • Boiling Points: Higher than alkanes but lower than alcohols of similar molecular weight
  • Solubility: Lower aldehydes are soluble in water; solubility decreases with chain length
  • Reactivity: Highly reactive due to electrophilic carbonyl carbon
  • Oxidation: Easily oxidized to carboxylic acids

IUPAC Nomenclature

Basic Naming Rules

  1. Find the longest chain containing the -CHO group
  2. The carbonyl carbon is always carbon 1 (no locant needed)
  3. Replace "-e" of alkane with "-al"
  4. Name substituents with their position numbers

Naming Format

// General format:
[substituents]-[parent]-al

// Examples:
methanal // 1 carbon aldehyde (formaldehyde)
ethanal // 2 carbon aldehyde (acetaldehyde)
propanal // 3 carbon aldehyde
2-methylpropanal // Branched aldehyde

Important: The aldehyde carbon is always numbered as carbon 1, so no locant is needed for the -CHO group. All other substituents are numbered from this position.

Common Aldehydes

Methanal (Formaldehyde)

  • Simplest aldehyde: HCHO
  • Used in preservatives and plastics
  • Highly reactive and toxic

Ethanal (Acetaldehyde)

  • Formula: CH3CHO
  • Intermediate in alcohol metabolism
  • Used in chemical synthesis

Benzaldehyde

  • Aromatic aldehyde with almond scent
  • Used in flavoring and perfumes
  • Formula: C6H5CHO

Common Reactions

Aldehydes are highly reactive and undergo various reactions:

  • Oxidation: Easily oxidized to carboxylic acids using mild oxidizing agents
  • Reduction: Reduced to primary alcohols using reducing agents like NaBH4
  • Nucleophilic Addition: Addition of nucleophiles to the carbonyl carbon
  • Aldol Condensation: Self-condensation to form β-hydroxy aldehydes
  • Acetal Formation: Reaction with alcohols to form acetals

Test for Aldehydes: Aldehydes give positive results with Tollens' reagent (silver mirror test) and Fehling's reagent, distinguishing them from ketones.
