Alkenes

General Formula: CnH2n

Unsaturated hydrocarbons with one or more C=C double bonds

What are Alkenes?

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon-carbon double bond. The double bond makes them more reactive than alkanes and is the site of most chemical reactions involving alkenes.

C=C

Double Bond

At least one C=C bond

sp2

Hybridization

Planar geometry at double bond

-ene

Suffix

Names end in "-ene"

Key Features

  • Hybridization: Carbon atoms in double bond are sp2 hybridized
  • Bond Angles: Trigonal planar geometry with 120° bond angles at double-bonded carbons
  • Pi Bond: Double bond consists of one sigma and one pi bond
  • Reactivity: Undergo addition reactions (hydrogenation, halogenation, hydration)
  • Restricted Rotation: No free rotation around double bond (leads to cis/trans isomerism)

IUPAC Nomenclature

Basic Naming Rules

  1. Find the longest chain containing the double bond
  2. Number from the end nearest to the double bond
  3. Indicate position of double bond with a locant before "-ene"
  4. Name substituents with their position numbers

Naming Format

// General format:
[substituents]-[parent]-[locant]-ene

// Examples:
prop-1-ene // Double bond at position 1
but-2-ene // Double bond at position 2
2-methylprop-1-ene // With substituent

Important: The locant number indicates the FIRST carbon of the double bond. "prop-1-ene" means the double bond is between carbons 1 and 2.

Isomerism in Alkenes

Structural Isomers

Different positions of the double bond create structural isomers:

  • But-1-ene: CH2=CH-CH2-CH3 - type but-1-ene
  • But-2-ene: CH3-CH=CH-CH3 - type but-2-ene

Geometric (Cis-Trans) Isomers

When there are different groups on each carbon of the double bond, cis and trans isomers exist:

  • Cis: Same groups on the same side of the double bond
  • Trans: Same groups on opposite sides of the double bond

Note: The renderer displays a simplified 3D model; cis/trans distinction may not be fully visualized.

Common Reactions

Alkenes undergo addition reactions where atoms add across the double bond:

  • Hydrogenation: Addition of H2 (requires catalyst)
  • Halogenation: Addition of Br2 or Cl2
  • Hydration: Addition of H2O (forms alcohols)
  • Hydrohalogenation: Addition of HBr or HCl
