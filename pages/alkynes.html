Alkynes

General Formula: CnH2n-2

Unsaturated hydrocarbons with one or more carbon-carbon triple bonds

What are Alkynes?

Alkynes are unsaturated hydrocarbons that contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond. The triple bond makes them highly reactive and gives them unique chemical properties distinct from both alkanes and alkenes.

Triple Bond

Hybridization

Suffix

Names end in "-yne"

Key Features

  • Hybridization: Carbon atoms in triple bond are sp hybridized
  • Bond Angles: Linear geometry with 180° bond angles at triple-bonded carbons
  • Bond Structure: Triple bond consists of one sigma and two pi bonds
  • Reactivity: More reactive than alkenes; undergo addition reactions
  • Acidity: Terminal alkynes (H-C≡C-) are weakly acidic
  • Bond Length: C≡C bond is shorter (1.20 A) than C=C (1.34 A) or C-C (1.54 A)

IUPAC Nomenclature

Basic Naming Rules

  1. Find the longest chain containing the triple bond
  2. Number from the end nearest to the triple bond
  3. Indicate position of triple bond with a locant before "-yne"
  4. Name substituents with their position numbers

Naming Format

// General format:
[substituents]-[parent]-[locant]-yne

// Examples:
prop-1-yne // Triple bond at position 1
but-2-yne // Triple bond at position 2
3-methylbut-1-yne // With substituent

Note: Ethyne (C2H2) is commonly known as acetylene. The simplest alkyne has only 2 carbons since a triple bond requires 2 carbon atoms.

Terminal vs Internal Alkynes

Terminal Alkynes

Have the triple bond at the end of the chain (position 1):

  • Structure: R-C≡C-H
  • Examples: ethyne, prop-1-yne, but-1-yne
  • The terminal hydrogen is weakly acidic (pKa ~25)

Internal Alkynes

Have the triple bond within the carbon chain:

  • Structure: R-C≡C-R'
  • Examples: but-2-yne, pent-2-yne
  • No acidic hydrogen; less reactive than terminal alkynes

Common Reactions

Alkynes undergo addition reactions similar to alkenes, but can add twice:

  • Hydrogenation: Addition of H2 - can form alkene or alkane
  • Halogenation: Addition of Br2 or Cl2 (up to 4 halogen atoms)
  • Hydration: Addition of H2O (forms ketones or aldehydes)
  • Hydrohalogenation: Addition of HX (follows Markovnikov's rule)

Tip: Partial hydrogenation of alkynes using Lindlar's catalyst produces cis-alkenes, while Na/NH3 produces trans-alkenes.
