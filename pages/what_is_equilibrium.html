What is Equilibrium

The state where forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates

What is Chemical Equilibrium?

Chemical equilibrium is the state in which the concentrations of reactants and products have no net change over time. This occurs when the forward and reverse reactions proceed at the same rate, creating a dynamic balance.

⇌

Forward and reverse reactions continue

=

Reaction rates are equal

🔄

Concentrations remain constant

Key Concepts

  • Dynamic Nature: Equilibrium is dynamic - reactions continue but concentrations remain constant
  • Reversible Reactions: Only reversible reactions can reach equilibrium
  • Equilibrium Constant (K): The ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium
  • Le Chatelier's Principle: Systems at equilibrium respond to disturbances by shifting to restore equilibrium
  • Homogeneous vs Heterogeneous: Equilibrium can occur in single phase or multiple phases

Equilibrium Constant (K)

Expression for K

For a general reaction: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

K = [C]^c × [D]^d / [A]^a × [B]^b

Types of Equilibrium Constants

Type Symbol Definition
ConcentrationKcBased on molar concentrations
PressureKpBased on partial pressures (gases)
ThermodynamicK°Standard equilibrium constant

Important: Solids and pure liquids are not included in equilibrium constant expressions because their concentrations are essentially constant.

Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to counteract the disturbance and restore equilibrium.

Factors Affecting Equilibrium

Change Effect on Equilibrium
Concentration increase (reactants)Shifts toward products
Concentration increase (products)Shifts toward reactants
Pressure increase (gases)Shifts toward fewer moles of gas
Temperature increase (exothermic)Shifts toward reactants
Temperature increase (endothermic)Shifts toward products
Catalyst additionNo shift, only affects rate

Common Equilibrium Reactions

Haber Process (Ammonia Synthesis)

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) + heat

Optimal conditions: High pressure, moderate temperature, iron catalyst

Contact Process (Sulfuric Acid)

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) + heat

Optimal conditions: High pressure, moderate temperature, vanadium(V) oxide catalyst

Water Autoionization

2H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq)

Kw at 25°C: 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴

Calculating Equilibrium Composition

ICE Table Method

Use ICE (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) tables to solve equilibrium problems:

// Example: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃
// Initial: [N₂] = 1.0 M, [H₂] = 3.0 M, [NH₃] = 0 M
// Change: -x, -3x, +2x
// Equilibrium: 1.0-x, 3.0-3x, 2x

// Use K expression to solve for x

Tip: Always check if the change (x) is small compared to initial concentrations to simplify calculations using the 5% rule.

Equilibrium and Gibbs Free Energy

The relationship between equilibrium constant and Gibbs free energy:

ΔG° = -RT ln(K)
  • K > 1: ΔG° is negative, products favored
  • K < 1: ΔG° is positive, reactants favored
  • K = 1: ΔG° = 0, equal amounts
