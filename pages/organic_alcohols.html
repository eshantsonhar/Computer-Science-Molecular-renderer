Alcohols

Functional Group: -OH (Hydroxyl)

Organic compounds containing a hydroxyl group attached to a carbon atom

What are Alcohols?

Alcohols are organic compounds characterized by a hydroxyl (-OH) functional group attached to a saturated carbon atom. They are one of the most important classes of organic compounds with wide applications in chemistry, industry, and everyday life.

Key Features

  • Hydrogen Bonding: Can form H-bonds, leading to higher boiling points than similar-sized hydrocarbons
  • Solubility: Lower alcohols (C1-C4) are miscible with water; solubility decreases with chain length
  • Polarity: The -OH group is polar, making alcohols polar molecules
  • Acidity: Weakly acidic (pKa ~16-18); can donate H+ from -OH group
  • Versatility: Can act as both weak acids and bases

Classification of Alcohols

Primary Alcohols (1°)

The -OH group is attached to a carbon with only one other carbon attached.

  • Structure: R-CH2-OH
  • Examples: methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol

Secondary Alcohols (2°)

The -OH group is attached to a carbon with two other carbons attached.

  • Structure: R2CH-OH
  • Examples: propan-2-ol, butan-2-ol

Tertiary Alcohols (3°)

The -OH group is attached to a carbon with three other carbons attached.

  • Structure: R3C-OH
  • Examples: 2-methylpropan-2-ol

IUPAC Nomenclature

Basic Naming Rules

  1. Find the longest chain containing the -OH group
  2. Number from the end nearest to the -OH group
  3. Replace "-e" of alkane with "-ol"
  4. Indicate position with a locant before "-ol"

Naming Format

// General format:
[substituents]-[parent]-[locant]-ol

// Examples:
methanol // 1 carbon alcohol
ethanol // 2 carbon alcohol
propan-1-ol // -OH at position 1
propan-2-ol // -OH at position 2
2-methylpropan-1-ol // Branched with -OH at C1

Priority: The -OH group gets priority over alkyl branches when numbering the carbon chain. Always number to give -OH the lowest possible locant.

Common Reactions

Alcohols undergo various reactions depending on their classification:

  • Oxidation: 1° → aldehyde → carboxylic acid; 2° → ketone; 3° → no reaction
  • Dehydration: Removal of H2O to form alkenes (needs acid catalyst + heat)
  • Esterification: Reaction with carboxylic acids to form esters
  • Halogenation: Replacement of -OH with halogen using HX or PX3

Oxidation Pattern: The ease of oxidation depends on the alcohol type. Primary alcohols oxidize easily, secondary alcohols oxidize to ketones, and tertiary alcohols resist oxidation.
