What are Halogenated Compounds?

Halogenated organic compounds (also called organohalides or alkyl halides) are organic compounds containing one or more halogen atoms (F, Cl, Br, I) bonded to carbon atoms. These compounds are important in industry, pharmaceuticals, and as synthetic intermediates.

Key Features

  • Polarity: C-X bonds are polar due to electronegativity differences
  • Reactivity: Good leaving groups in substitution and elimination reactions
  • Boiling Points: Generally higher than corresponding alkanes due to dipole interactions
  • Solubility: Generally insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents
  • Stability: C-F bonds are very strong; C-I bonds are relatively weak
  • Electronegativity Order: F > Cl > Br > I (affects bond polarity)

IUPAC Nomenclature

Basic Naming Rules

  1. Halogens are named as substituents using prefixes
  2. Find the longest carbon chain
  3. Number to give halogens lowest possible numbers
  4. List halogens alphabetically with other substituents

Naming Format

// General format:
[locant]-[halogen prefix][parent name]

// Examples:
chloromethane // CH3Cl
1-chloropropane // CH3CH2CH2Cl
2-chloropropane // CH3CHClCH3
1,2-dichloroethane // CH2ClCH2Cl

Multiple Halogens: Use prefixes di-, tri-, tetra- for multiple identical halogens. Different halogens are listed alphabetically (bromo before chloro).

Classification by Carbon Type

Primary Halides (1°)

Halogen attached to a primary carbon (bonded to only one other carbon):

  • Structure: R-CH2-X
  • Examples: 1-chloropropane, 1-bromobutane
  • Generally undergo SN2 reactions

Secondary Halides (2°)

Halogen attached to a secondary carbon (bonded to two other carbons):

  • Structure: R2CH-X
  • Examples: 2-chloropropane, 2-bromobutane
  • Can undergo both SN1 and SN2 reactions

Tertiary Halides (3°)

Halogen attached to a tertiary carbon (bonded to three other carbons):

  • Structure: R3C-X
  • Examples: 2-chloro-2-methylpropane
  • Primarily undergo SN1 reactions

Common Halogenated Compounds

Chloroform (CHCl3)

  • Systematic name: trichloromethane
  • Former anesthetic, now industrial solvent
  • Toxic and carcinogenic

Carbon Tetrachloride (CCl4)

  • Systematic name: tetrachloromethane
  • Former dry cleaning solvent
  • Ozone-depleting substance

Freons (CFCs)

  • Chlorofluorocarbons used as refrigerants
  • Example: CCl2F2 (dichlorodifluoromethane)
  • Banned due to ozone depletion

Common Reactions

Halogenated compounds are versatile synthetic intermediates:

  • Nucleophilic Substitution: SN1 and SN2 mechanisms replace halogen with nucleophile
  • Elimination: E1 and E2 mechanisms remove HX to form alkenes
  • Grignard Formation: React with Mg to form organometallic reagents
  • Reduction: Halogen can be replaced with hydrogen
  • Coupling Reactions: Form C-C bonds in organometallic chemistry

Leaving Group Ability: The order of leaving group ability is I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻. Iodides react fastest in substitution reactions, while fluorides are least reactive.
