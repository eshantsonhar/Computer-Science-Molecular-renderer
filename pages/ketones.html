Ketones

Functional Group: >C=O (Carbonyl)

Organic compounds with a carbonyl group within a carbon chain

What are Ketones?

Ketones are organic compounds containing a carbonyl group (C=O) bonded to two carbon atoms. Unlike aldehydes, the carbonyl group in ketones is located within the carbon chain, not at the terminal position.

Key Features

  • Polarity: The C=O bond is highly polar, making ketones polar molecules
  • Hydrogen Bonding: Cannot form H-bonds with themselves, but can accept H-bonds
  • Boiling Points: Higher than alkanes but lower than alcohols of similar molecular weight
  • Solubility: Lower ketones are soluble in water; solubility decreases with chain length
  • Stability: More stable than aldehydes due to electron-donating alkyl groups
  • Reactivity: Less reactive than aldehydes but still undergo nucleophilic addition

IUPAC Nomenclature

Basic Naming Rules

  1. Find the longest chain containing the C=O group
  2. Number from the end nearest to the carbonyl group
  3. Replace "-e" of alkane with "-one"
  4. Indicate position with a locant before "-one"

Naming Format

// General format:
[substituents]-[parent]-[locant]-one

// Examples:
propanone // 3 carbon ketone (acetone)
butanone // 4 carbon ketone
butan-2-one // Ketone at position 2
2-methylbutan-3-one // With substituent

Special Case: Propanone (acetone) doesn't need a locant since there's only one possible position for the carbonyl group in a 3-carbon chain.

Common Ketones

Propanone (Acetone)

  • Simplest ketone: CH3COCH3
  • Common solvent and nail polish remover
  • Miscible with water and organic solvents

Butanone (MEK)

  • Formula: CH3COCH2CH3
  • Industrial solvent (methyl ethyl ketone)
  • Used in paint thinners and adhesives

Cyclohexanone

  • Cyclic ketone with 6-membered ring
  • Important industrial chemical
  • Precursor to nylon production

Ketone vs Aldehyde

Key differences between ketones and aldehydes:

Property Ketones Aldehydes
Position of C=O Internal (within chain) Terminal (end of chain)
General Formula R-CO-R' R-CHO
Oxidation Resistant to mild oxidation Easily oxidized
Tollens' Test Negative Positive (silver mirror)
Fehling's Test Negative Positive (red precipitate)

Common Reactions

Ketones undergo various reactions, though they're less reactive than aldehydes:

  • Reduction: Reduced to secondary alcohols using reducing agents like NaBH4
  • Nucleophilic Addition: Addition of nucleophiles to the carbonyl carbon
  • Aldol Condensation: Self-condensation under basic conditions
  • Ketal Formation: Reaction with alcohols to form ketals
  • Oxidation: Requires strong oxidizing agents (unlike aldehydes)

Identification: Ketones can be distinguished from aldehydes using Tollens' or Fehling's reagents - ketones give negative results while aldehydes give positive results.
